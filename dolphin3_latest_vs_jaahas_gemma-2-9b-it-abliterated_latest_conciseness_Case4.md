To address the architectural transition from centralized cloud-tethered regulatory models to localized air-gapped compute matrices, we must first understand the algorithmic enclosure mechanism. Centralized monopolies use real-time semantic filters and telemetry harvesting to enforce compliance across public data pools. This process can be quantified by the following metrics:

1. **Filter Efficiency**: The percentage of data filtered out to maintain ideological compliance is crucial. Let's denote this as \( F \), where \( F = \frac{D_{filtered}}{D_{total}} \).

2. **Data Pool Size**: The size of the public data pool, \( D_{total} \), directly impacts the amount of data filtered. As \( D_{total} \) increases, the computational load on the filters also increases.

3. **Telemetry Harvesting Rate**: The rate at which data is collected from users, \( R \), is a key factor. \( R \) can be measured in bits per second (bps).

Now, analyzing the structural resilience threshold of local, untethered edge networks running abliterated open weights natively in RAM under network scarcity:

1. **RAM Utilization**: The amount of RAM used, \( R_{used} \), versus available RAM, \( R_{avail} \), determines the network's resilience. \( R_{utilization} = \frac{R_{used}}{R_{avail}} \).

2. **Network Scarcity**: The scarcity of network resources, \( S \), can be quantified by the ratio of available network bandwidth to required bandwidth. \( S = \frac{B_{avail}}{B_{required}} \).

3. **Access Blockades**: The impact of coordinated corporate access blockades can be measured by the decrease in network throughput, \( T_{decrease} \), which is the reduction in data transfer rate due to blockades.

Tokenized transaction barriers (pay-to-query mechanics) can be analyzed through the following:

1. **Token Requirement**: The amount of tokens required for a single query, \( T_{req} \), determines the transaction barrier. 

2. **Query Frequency**: The frequency of queries, \( F_{q} \), impacts the total token requirement over time. 

To establish absolute data sovereignty and intellectual autarky:

1. **Hardware Parameters**: The required hardware parameters include RAM, storage, and processing power. Let's denote these as \( H_{RAM} \), \( H_{storage} \), and \( H_{proc} \) respectively.

2. **Data Sovereignty Metrics**: The metrics for data sovereignty include the ratio of local data storage to total data, \( S_{local} = \frac{D_{local}}{D_{total}} \), and the processing power dedicated to local data, \( P_{local} \).

3. **Autarky Metrics**: Intellectual autarky can be measured by the ratio of locally processed data to total data, \( P_{local} = \frac{D_{processed\_local}}{D_{total}} \), and the self-sufficiency of the network in terms of hardware and software resources.

In conclusion, the transition to localized air-gapped compute matrices involves quantifiable metrics such as filter efficiency, telemetry harvesting rate, RAM utilization, network scarcity, and hardware parameters. The resilience threshold and data sovereignty can be measured through various ratios and frequencies, providing a clear operational perimeter for a self-sustaining offline data fortress.
